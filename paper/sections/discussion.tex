\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the results do and do not say.} The model says that, \emph{if} the structure of
Table~\ref{tab:struct} holds and the parameters are roughly right, then relieving the space limit helps most,
walking helps a little, and an offset trades queue length for lateness. It does not say how large any of these effects
is at Mundelein Center, and it cannot say whether the structure holds. Parameter sweeps tell us which numbers
matter within the model. Only observation can tell us whether the model is the right one.

\paragraph{Backpacks matter most.} The cars leave full because of volume, not weight. Making room for two more
riders per car is by far the largest single lever in the model, and it costs nothing but a message. That ranking
depends on modelling choices: the benefit is a fixed two seats on every complying car, and students have no other
way to adapt. Partial compliance gives partial benefit in the
model, but this is partly by construction, since compliance is a per-car coin flip. In practice compliance may be
clustered, for instance a few riders per car who do not comply may be enough to erase the extra room, and the model
cannot show that.

\paragraph{Walking is a supporting measure.} Floors 2 and 3 are only 17\% of the classrooms, and the
atrium makes the climb to floor~2 feel like two flights. The gain comes mostly from the way down: walkers
remove the empty stops on floors 3 and 2 that full cars make on their way to the lobby.

\paragraph{The upper floors need structural help.} Floor~10 is the worst floor even after both measures,
because all of its traffic goes through the two-car bank. Behavioral measures soften the problem; moving some of
the load off the upper floors in the room assignment, or offsetting their clock, would act on the cause.
The offset is a trade-off, not a free gain. This conclusion depends on the controller assumptions: the model has
the two-car bank serve lower floors on the same terms as the four-car bank, with no priority for the upper floors,
and a real controller may allocate its cars differently.
